## The bug Spencer found (diagnosed on his real plan)

In 2034 the home purchase needs about $1.0M from savings. Taxable investments opens the year at $796,207.

- The engine withdraws exactly the opening $796,207.
- The engine then credits the account with $66,059 of mid-year growth.
- That leaves $66K "left" while the year is reported $207,295 short.

The mid-year convention (docs/MATH.md) already treats a withdrawal as happening mid-year. Its close formula is exactly:

```
close = (open·(1+h) − W)·(1+h) + contributions·(1+h)
```

`h` is the half-period growth factor, `(1+r)^(f/2) − 1`, where `f` is the year fraction (1 for a full year).

So at the moment money is withdrawn, the account holds `open·(1+h)`, not `open`. Capping the withdrawal at `open` strands the half-year growth. **Withdrawal capacity must be `open·(1+h)`** for an account that grows. Once that's fixed, an account the waterfall drains closes at exactly 0.

The second gap: the year's unfunded shortfall simply vanishes, and the next year's surplus goes straight back into Taxable investments. Spencer wants it carried as a real deficit.

## E1 — Engine (`packages/engine/src/run.ts` + where the withdrawal waterfall's capacities are built; find `planWithdrawals` and how `waterfall` entries get their balance)

### 1. Withdrawal capacity at the mid-year balance
- For each account in the withdrawal waterfall whose balance grows (non-liability, growth rate > 0 this year), the amount available to withdraw is `balance × (1 + h)`.
- `h` is `(1+r)^(f/2) − 1`, using the same `r`, `f` and fee treatment the growth step uses. Reuse the existing helpers; don't re-derive.
- Tax gross-up and surrender charges keep working off the gross amount actually drawn.
- Growth stays `growthBase×full + contributions×half + withdrawals×(full − half)`. With `W = open·(1+h)` and no contributions, close is 0, within a cent.
- **Test it:** a $100,000 account at 21%, so `h` = 0.10 (√1.21 = 1.1), full year, needing $120,000. It withdraws $110,000, closes at 0, and is $10,000 unfunded.
- Annuity fees: the capacity uses GROSS growth (`h` is not netted of fees). The fee is charged on the post-draw balance and capped at what remains, so a drained contract pays no fee and closes at exactly 0. Stated in MATH.md.

### 2. Overdrawn balance carried forward
Add a per-plan running `overdrawn` balance, starting at 0.

- **Shortfall year:** after the waterfall runs dry, the unfunded remainder adds to `overdrawn`. `unfundedShortfall` keeps meaning *this year's new shortfall*, so existing consumers are unchanged.
- **Surplus year** (`net > 0`): repay `overdrawn` first, before `planAllocations`.
  - The repayment is `min(net, overdrawn)`. Only what's left is allocated to accounts and the sweep.
  - Paycheck and budget contributions are planned expenses that happen before `net`. They don't change.
  - Push an allocation line `{ label: 'Paying back overdrawn', amount, category: 'overdrawnRepayment' }` into `allocations` so readers can show it.
- **No interest** on the overdrawn balance. It's a planning signal, not a loan. Document it.
- **Snapshot.** Add an additive optional `overdrawn?: { open, added, repaid, close }` to `YearSnapshot`, present only when any of the four is non-zero.
  - `liabilities` and `netWorth` include `overdrawn.close`: liabilities += close, netWorth −= close.
  - Mention it in the field's doc comment.
- **Today's-dollars deflation** (`inflation.ts` `deflate`): `overdrawn.open` and `.close` are balances (balance deflator); `.added` and `.repaid` are flows (flow deflator, like `unfundedShortfall`), so today's-dollars `added` equals the deflated `unfundedShortfall`. `open + added − repaid ≠ close` in real dollars; B2 shows the residue as an "Inflation adjustment" sub-row.
- **Repaying year.** The year whose surplus brings the balance to 0 is not a short year: `added = 0`, `close = 0`, so it is not in `shortfallYears`.
- **Warnings** keep the existing per-year shortfall warning text.

### 3. Markers / health
In `packages/engine/src/markers.ts`, the shortfall years should be every year whose `overdrawn.close > 0`, plus any year with `unfundedShortfall > 0`. The red period lasts until the deficit is repaid. Keep `shortfallTotal` as the sum of new shortfalls (`unfundedShortfall`), and add `overdrawnPeak`, the max of `overdrawn.close`.
